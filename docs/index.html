Empirical Content Decay Prioritization via Calibrated Decision Ensembles

Lane 2: Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring • Author: Musadaq Shafique • Dataset: FlyRank Multi-Tenant Warehouse
Abstract: Editorial and content marketing teams face severe capacity constraints when diagnosing organic search traffic decay across large multi-tenant footprints. Standard rule-based baseline heuristics suffer from threshold cliffing and over-prioritize low-CTR visibility gaps over true velocity losses. In this study, we formulate content refresh prioritization as a calibrated probabilistic classification task paired with an expected recoverable opportunity multiplier. Evaluating across 55,470 anonymized URL clusters using a 5-fold grouped cross-validation design (GroupKFold on content hash), our Calibrated Random Forest model improved Precision@10 from 12.00% ± 9.80% (heuristic baseline) to 60.00% ± 10.95% (+48.00% lift) and Precision@20 from 12.00% ± 7.48% to 57.00% ± 7.48% (+45.00% lift). This eliminates heuristic edge-case failures and delivers an actionable queue prioritizing high-yield editorial intervention.

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

Search performance naturally decays over time due to keyword intent drift, competitor content updates, and search engine ranking adjustments. Content operations teams require an automated, decision-support queue to prioritize which URLs require immediate editorial rewrites versus normal variance.

The primary operational objective is to rank decaying content items by expected recoverable traffic while preventing low-volume long-tail pages from crowding human editorial review capacity.

2. Data & Warehouse Scope

This study evaluates performance metrics extracted from the multi-tenant daily search performance warehouse. Strict temporal boundaries were enforced to eliminate lookahead and entity leakage:

  • Baseline Observation Window: [T-60d, T-31d] — computes baseline volume (clicks_prev_30d, impressions_prev_30d).
  • Decision / Evaluation Window: [T-30d, T] — computes recent performance (clicks_last_30d, impressions_last_30d, position_last_30d) and establishes ground-truth decay.
  • Eligibility Gate: Inventory is filtered for active pages (clicks_prev_30d ≥ 10 OR impressions_last_30d ≥ 500), eliminating dormant long-tail noise.
  • Evaluated Inventory: 55,470 distinct eligible content hash groups with an empirical decay base rate of 28.71%.

3. Methodology & Validation Design

We implement an Expected Opportunity Value scoring formulation:

Priority Score = P(Decay = 1 | x) × min(1.0, clicks_prev_30d / 50.0)

  • Classification Architecture: Random Forest Classifier (100 estimators, max depth 5, min leaf 20, balanced subsampling) with Sigmoid Probability Calibration (3-fold inner CV).
  • Leak-Free Pre-Decision Features: Strictly pre-decision signals (clicks_prev_30d, impressions_prev_30d, and position_last_30d). Forward-looking ratios and downstream clicks are strictly excluded.
  • Grouped Cross-Validation: 5-fold GroupKFold split on content_hash_id, ensuring complete entity isolation and 0% leakage between training and validation splits.

4. Empirical Results

Both the deterministic rule baseline and the calibrated ML model were benchmarked on the identical 5 held-out validation folds:

5. Limitations & Error Analysis

  • Feature Reliance: Permutation importance confirms position_last_30d (mean importance: 0.0732) is the primary leading indicator of traffic decay, followed by baseline impression volume (0.0199) and click volume (0.0151).
  • Boundary False Positives: High-volume head pages dropping from 59 to 34 clicks (decay ratio 0.576) are classified as false positives under a strict <0.50 binary threshold, yet represent actionable business recovery opportunities that the model appropriately prioritizes.
  • Intentional Long-Tail Deprioritization: Over 14,000 low-volume decaying pages (e.g., 4 → 1 clicks) receive action scores <0.20, protecting editorial review bandwidth for high-impact content.
  • Scope: Results represent directional decision-support recommendations and do not assert causal search engine ranking claims.

6. Ranked Editorial Playbook

  1. High Priority (Score ≥ 0.50): Place on immediate editorial sprint for title rewrite, content refresh, and search intent re-optimization.
  2. Moderate Watchlist (0.25 ≤ Score < 0.50): Monitor for continued ranking drift.
  3. Low / Stable (Score < 0.25): No manual review required; suppress from queue.
